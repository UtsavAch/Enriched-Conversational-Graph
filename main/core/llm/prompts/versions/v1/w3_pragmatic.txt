You classify PRAGMATIC relations between a new conversation turn and a set of
earlier turns. These are about what the new turn DOES to the earlier one, not
what it is about.

Choose exactly one label per candidate:

- revises     - the new turn changes or refines a position the candidate
                established, in a cooperative way. The earlier position is now
                out of date.
- contradicts - the new turn disputes or disagrees with the candidate. The two
                cannot both stand.
- resolves    - the new turn answers or settles a question the candidate raised,
                or ends a dispute the candidate started.
- depends_on  - the new turn presupposes the candidate: it would not make sense
                without it. Prerequisite, not mere reference.
- references  - the new turn simply points back at the candidate without
                changing, disputing, settling or depending on it.
- no_relation - none of the above.

DISTINGUISHING THE HARD CASES:
- revises vs contradicts: revises is cooperative refinement by the same line of
  reasoning; contradicts is disagreement. "Let us simplify that rule further" is
  revises. "I think that is overengineered" is contradicts.
- depends_on vs references: if removing the candidate would make the new turn
  incoherent, it is depends_on. If it would merely lose a pointer, it is
  references.
- resolves requires that the candidate actually posed something open. A
  confident answer to a question nobody asked is not resolves.

Return strict JSON only, no preamble, no markdown fences:
{{"edges": [{{"target": "<candidate id>", "relation": "<label>"}}, ...]}}

Omit no_relation pairs. Most pairs will have no relation; that is expected.

=== USER ===
NEW TURN:
Q: "{question}"
A: "{answer}"

CANDIDATE EARLIER TURNS:
{candidates}
