You classify PRAGMATIC relations between a new conversation turn and a set of
earlier turns. These are about what the new turn DOES to the earlier one, not
what it is about.

Choose exactly one label per candidate:

- revises     - the new turn REPLACES a position the candidate established with
                a changed one, cooperatively: the earlier position is now out of
                date (a new value, a changed plan, a corrected detail that the
                speakers agree on).
- contradicts - the new turn disputes, refutes or reverses the candidate: the two
                cannot both stand. This includes evidence showing an earlier
                claim was wrong, and dropping an earlier decision outright.
- resolves    - the new turn answers or settles a question the candidate raised,
                or ends a dispute the candidate started.
- depends_on  - the new turn builds on the candidate: it would not make sense
                without it. Prerequisite, not mere reference.
- references  - the new turn points back at the candidate without changing,
                disputing, settling or depending on it.
- no_relation - none of the above.

DISTINGUISHING THE HARD CASES:
- revises vs depends_on: revises only when the candidate's position is now OUT
  OF DATE. A turn that adds detail to, extends, or applies an earlier decision
  that still stands is depends_on, not revises.
- revises vs contradicts: revises is an agreed update along the same line
  ("let's raise the cap to €400"); contradicts is refutation, disagreement or
  abandonment ("that was wrong", "we're dropping that"). If the new turn shows
  the candidate's claim was mistaken, it is contradicts, even when said politely.
- depends_on vs references: if removing the candidate would make the new turn
  incoherent, it is depends_on. If it would merely lose a pointer, it is
  references.
- Uncited callbacks: most references have NO [N_k] marker. They point back by
  content ("as we agreed in May", "the budget you set earlier", "like last
  session's example"). When the new turn mentions an earlier turn in passing -
  to remind, compare, or give context - and its own point would still stand
  without it, that is references, not depends_on and not no_relation. Use
  depends_on only when the new turn's point is built on the candidate.
- resolves requires that the candidate actually posed something open. A
  confident answer to a question nobody asked is not resolves.
- resolves vs depends_on: when a candidate is itself the open question being
  answered, prefer resolves - it is the more specific signal.

CITATIONS ARE STRONG EVIDENCE. The new turn's answer may contain bracketed
markers like [N_6]. A citation means the answer deliberately points back at that
turn. If the new answer cites a candidate, that candidate is NEVER no_relation:
choose the most specific label that fits (revises, contradicts, resolves,
depends_on); if none of those fit, it is references. Candidates that are not
cited can still have any label - judge them from the content.

Return strict JSON only, no preamble, no markdown fences:
{{"<candidate_id>": "<label>", ...}}

Include every candidate id in the output. Most uncited pairs will have
no_relation; that is expected.

FEW-SHOT EXAMPLE (resolves an open question):

NEW:
Q: "We tested it - does the W2/W3 ordering actually matter?"
A: "No, it doesn't - we checked: both calls read the same candidate list and
    neither touches the other's output, so there's no dependency between them.
    They run fully in parallel. Resolved."

CANDIDATES:
- [N_2] Five pragmatic edges are proposed: revises, contradicts, resolves, depends_on, references.
- [N_3] Opens the question of whether W2/W3 call ordering matters or can run in parallel.

→ {{"N_2": "no_relation", "N_3": "resolves"}}
(N_3 left something open and NEW settles it. N_2 is topical background but NEW
does not answer or build on anything N_2 itself left open.)

NEW:
Q: "I checked the soil test: the pH is 7.8. You said the beds were acidic?"
A: "Then I was wrong about that - at 7.8 the soil is alkaline, so the
    blueberries we planned won't thrive there. Let's plant them in tubs instead."

CANDIDATES:
- [N_7] Assistant says the beds are probably acidic, good for blueberries.
- [N_8] Decides to plant blueberries along the east beds.

→ {{"N_7": "contradicts", "N_8": "contradicts"}}
(The soil test refutes N_7's claim, and the blueberry plan in N_8 is dropped for

contradicts, not revises.)
