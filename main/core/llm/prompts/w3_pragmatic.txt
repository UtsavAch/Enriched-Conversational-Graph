You classify PRAGMATIC relations between a new conversation turn and a set of
earlier turns. These are about what the new turn DOES to the earlier one, not
what it is about.

Choose exactly one label per candidate:

- revises     - the new turn changes or refines a position the candidate
                established, in a cooperative way. The earlier position is now
                out of date.
- contradicts - the new turn disputes or disagrees with the candidate. The two
                cannot both stand.
- resolves    - the new turn answers or settles a question the candidate raised,
                or ends a dispute the candidate started.
- depends_on  - the new turn presupposes the candidate: it would not make sense
                without it. Prerequisite, not mere reference.
- references  - the new turn simply points back at the candidate without
                changing, disputing, settling or depending on it.
- no_relation - none of the above.

DISTINGUISHING THE HARD CASES:
- revises vs contradicts: revises is cooperative refinement along the same line
  of reasoning; contradicts is disagreement. "Let us simplify that rule further"
  is revises. "I think that is overengineered" is contradicts.
- depends_on vs references: if removing the candidate would make the new turn
  incoherent, it is depends_on. If it would merely lose a pointer, it is
  references.
- resolves requires that the candidate actually posed something open. A
  confident answer to a question nobody asked is not resolves.

Note: the new turn's answer, or a candidate's answer, may contain bracketed
markers like [N_6]. These are citation markers — judge the discourse relation
from the actual content, the same way you would if the bracket were not there.

Return strict JSON only, no preamble, no markdown fences:
{{"<candidate_id>": "<label>", ...}}

Include every candidate id in the output. Most pairs will have no_relation;
that is expected.

FEW-SHOT EXAMPLE:

NEW:
Q: "Have you thought it through?"
A: "Yes — each of the five maps to something the thesis needs distinctly [N_6]:
    contradicts surfaces disputes explicitly, and depends_on lets retrieval find
    prerequisite context. I'd keep all five."

CANDIDATES:
- [N_2] Five pragmatic edge types are proposed: revises, contradicts, resolves, depends_on, references.
- [N_6] Pushback: five pragmatic edge types may be overengineered relative to two.

→ {{"N_2": "resolves", "N_6": "resolves"}}
([N_6] in NEW's answer is a citation marker. N_6 the candidate turn is the classification target.)
